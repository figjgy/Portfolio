### 🎪 SPATIAL & 3D

### 3.1 — EROPTIX exhibition booth · 3D design
*SketchUp · Ruby scripting · Brand environments · **Lead with this — it photographs best***

- **Challenge** — Design a medical exhibition booth that would stop surgeons walking past,
  built to K-PICK brand standards, with a working demo station for a surgical robot.
- **What I did** — Modelled the full booth in SketchUp: curved back wall with a navy fascia
  and red accent band, brand columns, backlit signage panels, and a central demo table with
  the EROPTIX robot arm modelled in place. Built it parametrically through Ruby scripting
  rather than by hand, so dimensions, colours and layout could be revised in seconds instead
  of rebuilt. Brand palette locked to the K-PICK navy and red.
- **Result** — A booth that could be reviewed and revised in 3D before anything was fabricated.
- **Show** — 3D views from several angles, and the booth as built at SMX. Before-and-after
  is very strong here.

### 3.2 — Interior design & space planning
*SketchUp · Measured drawings · Materials take-off · Cost estimation*

- **Challenge** — Take a residential space from measured survey to a costed, buildable
  proposal.
- **What I did** — Modelled the space to exact measured dimensions in SketchUp. Produced a
  materials take-off from the model — real materials, real quantities, not a rough guess —
  and turned it into a costed estimate covering both materials and labour.
- **Result** — A design and a defensible number attached to it.
- **Show** — renders, plan views, and a clean page of the quantity schedule. The costing is
  the part most interior portfolios do not have. Do not skip it.
- **Note** — private project. Show the work, omit the client and the address.

### 3.3 — 3D product visualisation
*Blender · Product rendering · Technical marketing*

- **Challenge** — Medical syringes are small, transparent and nearly impossible to photograph
  well. Suppliers sent nothing usable.
- **What I did** — Built the products in Blender from real manufacturer specifications —
  10 ml Luer Lock, insulin variants at 6, 8 and 12.7 mm — and rendered turntables and
  transparent stills. Wrote an eight-scene animated product film with callouts and spec
  overlays. Added a compliance gate so the renderer refuses to output banned wording.
- **Result** — Marketing visuals for products with no photography.
- **Show** — the renders. Let them speak.

### 🎬 CONTENT & VIDEO

### 3.4 — PhilMed Expo 2026 · Full exhibit content operation
*Event marketing · Video production · Lead capture · SMX Manila, 19–21 August 2026*

- **Challenge** — Two booths, three days, a brand nobody in the room knew, and a medical
  audience that ignores consumer marketing.
- **What I did** — Three-day content plan with per-date scripts written before the event. A
  shooting guide for the DJI Osmo Pocket 4 and a booth production bible. Shot and edited
  on-site coverage for both booths. Ran the follow-up — recap content, thank-you posts, and
  a structured lead process.
- **Result** — 91 leads captured and organised into a tiered follow-up system with owners
  and next actions, not a stack of business cards.
- **Show** — booth footage, recap reel, the lead tracker.

### 3.5 — Three-brand content calendar system
*Content strategy · Notion · Editorial systems*

- **Challenge** — Three brands, completely different audiences: TikTok shoppers, smart-lock
  buyers, hospital procurement officers. All needing daily content, all previously ad-hoc.
- **What I did** — A Notion calendar system covering all three, with a fixed weekly theme
  structure for the medical brand so posting never starts from a blank page. Wired it to
  auto-push each day's task to the social media coordinator. Planned around PH commerce
  moments — 8.8, 9.9, payday, ber-months.
- **Result** — ~30 posts per brand per month, scheduled a month ahead, with medical
  compliance rules built into the system.
- **Show** — calendar views, the theme system, a month laid out.

### 3.6 — Short-form video scripting
*TikTok · Reels · Facebook · Scriptwriting*

- **Challenge** — Kokolock needed a relaunch after going quiet. It had to work for a Filipino
  audience without sounding like an ad.
- **What I did** — 18 scripts for a single relaunch week in a four-line
  hook/problem/solution/CTA format with per-scene breakdowns, so any shooter can execute
  them. Taglish hooks, trend-aware formats.
- **Result** — A repeatable format now used across all three brands.
- **Show** — a script page beside the finished video.

### ⚙️ SYSTEMS & AUTOMATION

### 3.7 — K-PICK Multimedia Hub
*Flask · Notion API · Role-based access · **The unexpected one***

- **Challenge** — Content, leads, tasks and attendance lived in four places. Korean
  leadership could not see progress without asking.
- **What I did** — A web dashboard with login and role-based access — manager, member and
  viewer each see something different. Content calendar, lead tracker, task board, requests
  board, daily log, attendance. Notion as the backend, with caching and pagination so it
  stays fast as the data grows.
- **Result** — Leadership checks the dashboard instead of asking.
- **Show** — dashboard screenshots. Say plainly that you built it.

### 3.8 — The automation stack
*Python · Telegram Bot API · Process automation · Monitoring*

- **Challenge** — Reporting, monitoring and admin were eating hours that should have gone
  to content.
- **What I did** — Five services running in production:
  - **Reporting bot** — writes the daily end-of-day report to Telegram automatically
  - **Desktop activity tracker** — turns screen activity into task lines for the report,
    with idle detection and a privacy filter so personal work never appears
  - **Watchdog** — monitors the other services and restarts what fails
  - **Nightly backup** — automatic, versioned
  - **Mail sync** — inbox to dashboard with summaries and Korean translation
- **Result** — The daily report writes itself. Single-instance locks and a supervisor keep
  the stack up without babysitting.
- **Show** — an architecture diagram and a sample auto-generated report. This is the most
  senior-looking thing in the portfolio.
